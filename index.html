The cost of a 0.5% methane leakage rate

Measured leakage from US oil and gas is about 2%. Cutting it to 0.5% by 2035 would cost about $3.6 billion a year, and the Waste Emissions Charge as written would not get there.

Fee $1,211/t, 2035

Each row adds one feature of the law to a fee on every measured tonne. The bar shows how much of the needed cut each version delivers. Even at $50,000 a tonne the WEC as written stops near 1.40%.

Once EPA's methane rules are in force in every state, covered sites are exempt from the charge; with the 2024 rules in place it would cut only another 33,000 tonnes. The WEC's start moved to 2034 in July 2025, Congress struck down EPA's implementing rule in March 2025, and EPA has proposed pausing the reporting the charge depends on until 2034.

What's included

Methane from oil and gas production, gathering, processing, and transmission and storage, including unburned fuel from compressor engines. Local distribution is left out. Alaska's reinjected gas is not counted as production.

How the baseline works

Each basin's latest measured loss rate is held at today's level and applied to its 2035 production from EIA's AEO2026. Basins without a measurement campaign use EDF's measurement-based inventory, EI-ME.

How costs work

Twenty-five measures cut emissions in sequence within 14 source categories in each of 19 basins. Costs are annualized at 7%, start from today's equipment with no rules assumed, and net out the gas kept at AEO2026 2035 regional prices.

Limitations

  • Measured rates held flat. Basin rates stay at their latest measurement to 2035. Operators already cutting would lower the baseline and the cost; a 20% lower baseline cuts the cost to $2.4 billion a year.
  • Measure performance. Effectiveness comes from EPA, ICF and field studies. If every measure works 25% less well, the cost rises to $18 billion a year.
  • Plugging. Without plugging low-producing wells, the cost rises to $7.8 billion a year and many basins cannot reach the target.
  • Reported emissions under the WEC. How much of a fixed leak shows up in a company's reported emissions is an assumption, set lower for large leaks and higher for sources EPA's reporting rule estimates with factors.
  • Rules mapped to measures. The share of each measure that EPA's rules force is an assessment of the rule text, shown in the workbook.
  • No market effects. Gas prices and production do not respond to the cost of controls or to a fee.
  • Collection method. Records were collected with the use of AI and authors, checking second sources when available.
